Openings: the first view

1 The first view leads with one line to learn, one turn a line, each with one sentence from the book's facts: how the line ends, and how the other book moves end. A new player needs a start and its reason, not a table of numbers.

2 The book's source and the switch "Show the values" sit in the title row, above the table, so that they stay in place as the table changes height. The values stay closed until the player asks, and the browser keeps the choice. The book has no counts of games, so the line says where its order comes from.

3 "Book" means the moves that the engine studied, not only good ones. So the fine moves (Good or better) have their own group, and the book moves that the deeper values made worse follow under "Other book moves".

4 The live engine waits at a book position: the book's searches are deeper. "Candidates" in the table's switch starts it, and "Book" stops it.

5 KataGo's "other" row: the best move outside the book, so the player sees how far the rest is from the book's moves.

6 The book's tree in the move list: the main line, and one folded row under each move that has other book moves. Each chevron is a button that says whether it is open. A turn's two edges in either order are one branch.

7 The board numbers the book's moves as the table does, as the review draws the engine's candidates. The pointer on a row highlights its edge on the board and draws its line. The pointer on a numbered edge highlights its row.

Openings: a position with traps

1 The path from the start stays open, from where it leaves the main line. Every other branch is folded. The position on screen is the move list's current item.

2 The traps are a group of their own, under the book moves, the most tempting first. A book move that is a trap (A10-C11) moves into this group.

3 Why a trap tempts, in its own cell, with the values open or closed: its value at a short look (32 visits) and at the deep look. No number is set in bold, so the trap never reads as the strongest move.

4 The refutation has its own row with its line, so the player reads it before playing it. One action plays the trap and its refutation, edge by edge, with the step controls.

5 The title row names the turn, the side to move and the edge of the turn, then the position's symbol and its bounds.

6 One sentence under the table explains the best move, from the book's facts. The pointer or the focus on another row shows that row's sentence in its place.

7 Transpositions: in the third prototype's book, all 31 positions that two move orders reach are a turn's two edges in the other order. None comes from another turn. So "Also reached by" shows only for move orders that differ across turns, and this book has none.

Review: where the game left the book

1 One row in the Report answers "where did my game leave the book". A click goes to the position before the move that left it, and turns the book on for this review only. When the game played a book move worse than Good before it left, the row names that move too.

2 One mark, at the game's last book move (L10-N11). Every move keeps its review label. The moves after the mark have no book mark.

3 The book's table takes the candidates' place. "Candidates" shows the review's candidates for the same position. Its name differs from the engine header's switch.

4 The game's move keeps the book's columns for the book's values: the book has none for it. The move cell gives the review's label and value, with their source. Here the game left the book with a Good move: a book move is a move that the engine studied, and the book does not hold every fine move.

Analysis board: a position outside the book

1 Outside the book, one line in the title row, not a box, names the last book position on the path, with a link to it and to Openings. The engine's candidates show under it.

2 The switch shows what the table holds: "Candidates" is chosen, and "Book" is unavailable, with "Not in the book" as its reason. The player's choice of "Book" comes back at the next book position.
